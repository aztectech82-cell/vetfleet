How to Get Your Fleet Compliant with Clean Truck CheckCalifornia's heavy-duty inspection and maintenance program

A step-by-step checklist from a CARB-credentialed Clean Truck Check tester. Clean Truck Check was previously called Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance (HD I/M).

1. Find every truck that is covered

The program covers diesel and alternative-fuel vehicles over 14,000 lb GVWR operating in California. That includes owner-operators, out-of-state trucks, and federal, state, and local government vehicles. Build one list with each truck's VIN, plate and state, GVWR, fuel type, and engine model year.

2. Report the trucks and pay the fee in CTC-VIS

Each fleet reports its vehicles and pays an annual per-vehicle compliance fee in CARB's Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System (CTC-VIS). Keep it current when trucks are added, sold, or retired. Pay only through CTC-VIS, and check the current fee there.

3. Know which test each truck gets

  • OBD test: 2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines, including hybrids. No MIL-related issues can be present.
  • Opacity test: older engines without OBD get an SAE J1667 smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions control equipment.

Tests must be done by a CARB-credentialed tester. The one exception is an OBD test submitted by a telematics provider using a CARB-verified device.

4. Put every deadline on one calendar

Most vehicles test twice a year. California-registered trucks have deadlines that line up with their DMV registration. Out-of-state, exempt-plated, and federal vehicles go by the last digit of the VIN. CARB has scheduled OBD-equipped vehicles to move to four tests a year in October 2027, which doubles the number of test dates to track.

Rule of thumb: set reminders 60 and 30 days ahead of each truck's deadline, and do not dispatch a truck in California with an overdue test or an unresolved notice.

5. Keep emissions equipment healthy between tests

Most failures trace back to the same systems: the DPF and its regeneration history, SCR and DEF, EGR, NOx sensors, and exhaust or intake leaks. Have drivers report warning lights, derates, failed regenerations, and smoke right away. Do not clear fault codes to get through a test. Clearing codes resets the monitors, and a truck with incomplete monitors will not pass.

6. Handle a failed test the right way

  1. Get the fault data from the failed test.
  2. Diagnose the cause before replacing parts.
  3. Repair, then retest with a credentialed tester.
  4. Confirm the passing result shows in CTC-VIS, and keep the test, repair, and retest records.

7. Answer CARB notices inside 30 days

CARB's roadside monitoring network flags possible high emitters. The owner gets a Notice to Submit to Testing and has 30 days to submit a passing test. Missing it is an enforcement violation. Compliance is also linked to DMV registration, so an unresolved problem can stop a renewal.

8. Check the other CARB rules

Clean Truck Check is one program. Depending on the fleet, the Truck and Bus Regulation, Advanced Clean Fleets, drayage rules, and transport refrigeration unit rules may also apply. If you are not sure which ones cover you, ask.

Common questions

Which trucks need Clean Truck Check?
Diesel and alternative-fuel vehicles over 14,000 lb GVWR that operate in California, including out-of-state trucks and public fleet vehicles. Diesel and alternative-fuel hybrids are included.

How often does each truck have to be tested?
Most vehicles test twice a year. CARB has scheduled OBD-equipped vehicles to move to four tests a year starting in October 2027. California-registered trucks have deadlines tied to their DMV registration dates; out-of-state trucks go by the last digit of the VIN.

What test does my truck get?
2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines get an OBD test. Older engines without OBD get an SAE J1667 smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions control equipment.

I got a Notice to Submit to Testing. What now?
A Notice to Submit to Testing gives you 30 days to submit a passing compliance test. Call us with the notice in hand and we will schedule the test. If the truck fails, we diagnose the fault so it can be repaired and retested inside the window.
